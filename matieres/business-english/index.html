1. Workplace communication

Communication is the exchange of information, ideas and feelings. At work, it helps people coordinate tasks, carry out plans, make decisions, solve problems and build professional relationships.

2. Misunderstanding gap

A misunderstanding gap is the difference between what the sender intends and what the receiver understands. For example, “Please finish this ASAP” gives no exact deadline: one person may understand “today” and another “tomorrow”. Give a precise date and time instead.

3. Five components of communication

Sender: creates and sends the message. Message: the information communicated. Channel: the method used, such as an email or a call. Receiver: interprets the message. Feedback: a response that helps verify understanding.

4. Common communication barriers

Environmental: noise, distraction or distance. Language: jargon or unfamiliar words. Psychological: stress, lack of attention or assumptions. Context and tone: irony or an unclear situation. Intermediary: information changed or omitted when passed on.

5. Verbal and non-verbal communication

Verbal: communication using spoken or written words. Non-verbal: facial expressions, eye contact, gestures and posture. Tone and context can also affect the way words are understood.

6. Consequences and good practices

Misunderstandings can cause delays, repeated work, conflict, wasted resources and loss of trust. To prevent them, use clear words, adapt to the audience, listen, ask questions and request feedback.

Exam takeaway

Sending a message does not prove it was understood. Check what the receiver understood, especially when tasks and deadlines matter.
